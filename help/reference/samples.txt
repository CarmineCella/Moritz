# Samples: buffers, players, recorders, sound files

@ buffer~
syntax: buffer~ name [file] [ms] [channels]
Named sample memory. Every object that names it (play~, groove~, record~,
index~, poke~, waveform~, conv~, sparkle~, pursuit, soundtypes...) reads or
writes the same samples; names are shared by all open patches, as in Max. A
file given as argument is read when the patch opens; otherwise the buffer is
empty, or `ms` long at the audio's sample rate. Right-click it to edit, load,
save or clear it.

Double-click it (locked), or any object that names it, to open the sound
editor: an overview, a lane of regions and one of markers, a waveform per
channel and the spectrogram (Wave, Spectrum or Both), zoomed with Cmd+ and
Cmd-, Cmd and the wheel or a trackpad's pinch, scrolled with the wheel or the
overview. Drag to select (Shift extends the selection, Cmd+A selects all),
click to set the playhead; Space plays the selection, or from the playhead,
through the audio output beside the patches (Cmd+L loops it). The Edit menu
(or the right button) cuts, copies, pastes (over the selection, or at the
playhead; a sound at another rate is resampled), deletes, trims to the
selection, silences, fades in or out, changes the gain, normalizes the peak to
0 dB and reverses; each edit is one step of Undo (Cmd+Z). The Draw tool, zoomed
in until each sample has a pixel, moves samples with the mouse (one undo step
a stroke).
The Process menu runs an offline object over the selection (all of the sound
when nothing is selected), with a dialog for its settings, as a background job
(Esc stops it): pursuit (rebuilt with atoms: Gabor, gammatone or an atoms
object), typematch (by the units of another sound), hpss (the harmonic or the
percussive part), nmf (some of its parts), pvoc (stretched or transposed),
sparkle~ (cross-synthesis with another sound), filterdesign's filters, condec
(convolved with a response). A patch... renders the selection offline through
a .moritz patch, in through its inlet~s and out through its outlet~s; A Musil
script... calls the function (process x sr) a .mu file defines, x a list of
channel vectors, sr the rate, which returns a vector or a list of channel
vectors (examples/14-sound-editor has some). The result replaces the selection
(one undo step) or opens as a new sound (Result in the menu).
Markers: M puts one at the playhead, the Marker tool where it clicks; drag one
in its lane, right-click it to rename or delete it. The Markers menu places
them from onsets, beats or novelty, and makes regions from the selection, from
soundtypes' units (colored by type) or between onsets. The regions table lists
them (name, start, end, type, energy, pitch): a click selects one, a
double-click renames it; To coll... writes them as rows (Musil data, for a
coll), To score... as a .score with a sound event for each region, which a
score object reads and plays. Markers and regions are edits as the others (Undo takes them back).
The buffer~ changes at once for every object that uses it, record~ and poke~
writing into it are seen as they write; it is patch memory, so nothing is
marked unsaved, its markers stay with it while the patch is open, and Save
writes a copy as a WAV file (its markers beside it). Copied samples paste into
any sound editor (a sound with other channels is mixed down or spread). File >
Open Sound... edits a sound file the same way, as a document of its own that
Save writes back (24-bit WAV), its markers and regions in a file beside it
(sound.wav.markers, Musil data).
arg name: the name the other objects use (required)
arg file: a sound file read when the patch opens: WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3, relative to the patch (or among the bundled sounds)
arg ms: the length of an empty buffer, in milliseconds (0)
arg channels: the channels of an empty buffer, 1 to 8 (1)
msg read file: reads a sound file; the buffer takes its length, channels and sample rate
msg write file: writes the buffer as a WAV file
msg size ms: changes the length, keeping what fits and filling with silence
msg clear: sets every sample to 0
see: play~ groove~ record~ waveform~ index~ poke~

@ play~
syntax: play~ name [channels]
Plays a buffer~ once, at its own sample rate, interpolating between samples.
`start` or bang plays it from the beginning, or from `from` to `to` (ms).
With a signal connected to its inlet it plays nothing by itself: it reads the
buffer at that position in ms, for example from line~. Double-click it
(locked) to edit the buffer~ in the sound editor.
arg name: the buffer~ to play (required)
arg channels: the number of outlets, 1 to 8 (1); a mono buffer plays on all of them
msg start [from [to]]: plays from `from` (ms, 0) to `to` (ms; the end when missing or 0)
msg bang: plays the whole buffer
msg stop: stops
msg set name: plays another buffer~
see: buffer~ groove~ index~ line~ waveform~ sfplay~

@ groove~
syntax: groove~ name [channels]
Variable-speed playback of a buffer~: the signal in the left inlet is the
rate (1 as recorded, 0.5 an octave down, negative backwards). Without looping
it stops at either end; with `loop 1` it cycles between the loop start and
end (inlets 2 and 3, ms). The last outlet is a sync ramp, 0 to 1 through the
loop. Double-click it (locked) to edit the buffer~ in the sound editor.
arg name: the buffer~ to play (required)
arg channels: the number of audio outlets, 1 to 8 (1)
msg number: jumps to that position (ms); in inlets 2 and 3, the loop start and end (ms; an end of 0, or not after the start, is the end of the buffer)
msg loop 1/0: turns looping on or off (off)
msg startloop: jumps to the loop start
msg set name: plays another buffer~
see: play~ buffer~ sig~ record~ sparkle~

@ record~
syntax: record~ name [channels]
Records its signal inlets into a buffer~, from its beginning; the buffer
keeps its length, so give it one (buffer~ name file, or buffer~ name ms).
Recording stops at the end unless `loop 1` makes it wrap around. A buffer
with more channels than inlets takes the inlets again in turn. Double-click
it (locked) to edit the buffer~ in the sound editor.
arg name: the buffer~ to record into (required)
arg channels: the number of signal inlets, 1 to 8 (1)
msg 1 / 0: 1 starts recording from the beginning, 0 stops (left inlet)
msg loop 1/0: wraps to the beginning at the end instead of stopping (off)
msg set name: records into another buffer~
see: buffer~ play~ groove~ waveform~ sfrecord~ poke~

@ sfplay~
syntax: sfplay~ [channels]
Plays a sound file without a buffer~: `open` reads the whole file into the
object's own memory, then 1 plays it from the start (from the end when the
speed is negative). Right-click it and choose Open file... to pick the file.
arg channels: the number of outlets, 1 to 8 (1); a mono file plays on all of them
msg open file: reads a WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3 file, relative to the patch
msg 1 / 0: 1 plays from the start, 0 stops
msg bang: plays from the start
msg loop 1/0: loops the file (off)
msg speed x: the playback speed (1); negative plays backwards
see: play~ buffer~ sfrecord~ groove~

@ sfrecord~
syntax: sfrecord~ [channels]
Records its signal inlets to a 24-bit WAV file at the audio's sample rate.
`open` names the file, 1 starts and 0 stops; the file is complete once it
stops. Audio must be on: it records once audio starts. The outlet reports
the recorded time in ms, every 250 ms and when it stops. A writer thread
saves the file, so recording never holds up the audio. Right-click it and
choose Choose file... to pick the file.
arg channels: the number of signal inlets (and channels of the file), 1 to 8 (1)
msg open file.wav: the file to write, relative to the patch (it is overwritten)
msg 1 / 0: 1 starts recording, 0 stops and closes the file
msg start: starts recording
msg stop: stops and closes the file
see: sfplay~ record~ buffer~ dac~

@ waveform~
syntax: waveform~ name [width] [height]
Shows a buffer~, one lane per channel, with its name and length; it follows
the buffer while record~ writes into it. In locked mode, drag across it to
select a region: the outlets give its start and end in ms.
arg name: the buffer~ to show (required)
arg width: the width in pixels, 80 to 2000 (320)
arg height: the height in pixels, 40 to 1000 (110)
msg number: draws a cursor at that time (ms)
msg select start end: sets the selection (ms) and outputs it
msg set name: shows another buffer~
see: buffer~ play~ record~ plot

@ count~
syntax: count~ [min] [max] [loop]
A sample counter: its output goes up by one every sample. bang starts it
from `min`, a number from that value; `stop` stops it and the output holds
where it stopped (`min` before the first start). At `max` it wraps to `min`,
or stops there when `loop` is 0. Inside pfft~ a start falls on a frame
boundary, so the count stays aligned to the bins.
arg min: the start value (0)
arg max: the value where it wraps or stops; 0, or not above min, counts on forever (0)
arg loop: 1 wraps at max, 0 stops there (1)
msg bang: counts from min
msg number: counts from that value
msg stop: stops counting
see: index~ poke~ phasor~ line~ sah~

@ index~
syntax: index~ name [channel]
Reads one channel of a buffer~ at the sample index in its inlet (a signal,
or a number), without interpolation; indices outside the buffer read 0.
Double-click it (locked) to edit the buffer~ in the sound editor.
arg name: the buffer~ to read (required)
arg channel: the channel to read, from 1 (1)
msg set name: reads another buffer~
see: poke~ count~ buffer~ play~

@ poke~
syntax: poke~ name [channel]
Writes the signal in its left inlet into one channel of a buffer~, at the
sample index in its right inlet (signals or numbers); indices outside the
buffer are ignored. Double-click it (locked) to edit the buffer~ in the sound editor.
arg name: the buffer~ to write (required)
arg channel: the channel to write, from 1, at most the buffer's channels (1)
msg set name: writes into another buffer~
see: index~ count~ record~ buffer~

@ condec
syntax: condec
Convolution and deconvolution of whole buffer~s, computed at once when asked
(the author's condec; the deconvolution is Musil's deconv). The result replaces the contents of the out buffer~,
at the sample rate of the first; channels pair up, and the buffer with fewer
channels repeats its last. Deconvolution divides the spectrum of y by that of
h where h is above a threshold, so a recording can be "un-reverberated" by its
impulse response. The outlet bangs when the result is in.
msg conv x h out [scale] [mix]: writes x convolved with h into out (length x + h - 1), times scale (1), plus x times mix (0)
msg deconv y h out [threshold] [scale]: writes y deconvolved by h into out (the length of y): bins where h is below threshold (0.001) times its peak are set to 0; times scale (1)
see: conv~ buffer~ reverb~ render

@ render
syntax: render [patch] [channels]
Renders another patch offline, faster than real time, into a buffer~, as a
background job. Each render starts the patch afresh at the audio's sample
rate (loaded, the stored sends applied, loadbang) and records its dac~. The
rendered patch has its own send/receive and buffer~ names; its printed output
appears when the render ends. Renders asked for while one runs wait their
turn, with the sends and input of the moment they were asked for.
arg patch: the patch file to render, relative to this patch
arg channels: the dac~ channels recorded into the buffer~, 1 to 16 (2)
msg open patch.moritz: chooses the patch to render
msg send name value...: sends the value to the patch's receive name before every render (kept until clear)
msg input buffer: feeds the buffer~ to the patch's adc~
msg render buffer seconds: renders that many seconds into the buffer~, which takes their length and channels
msg clear: forgets the sends and the input
msg stop: cancels the render and the ones waiting
see: buffer~ poly~ play~ condec
