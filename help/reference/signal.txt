# Signal: Oscillators, Filters, Signal Math, Signal Routing, Delay, I/O

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Oscillators

@ cycle~
syntax: cycle~ [freq] [phase]
Cosine oscillator, read from a 512-point table with linear interpolation, as
in Max. The left inlet takes the frequency as a signal or a float; a float in
the right inlet restarts the cycle at that phase.
arg freq: the frequency in Hz (0)
arg phase: the starting phase, 0-1 (0)
see: phasor~ saw~ rect~ tri~ sig~

@ phasor~
syntax: phasor~ [freq]
A ramp from 0 to 1, repeating at the frequency in the left inlet (signal or
float). It is not band-limited: use it as an LFO, a position signal or an
index, not as an audible sawtooth (see saw~). A float in the right inlet
restarts the ramp at that phase (0-1).
arg freq: the frequency in Hz (0)
see: cycle~ saw~ line~ index~

@ saw~
syntax: saw~ [freq]
Band-limited sawtooth (PolyBLEP), from -1 to 1. The frequency is a signal or
a float.
arg freq: the frequency in Hz (0)
see: rect~ tri~ phasor~ cycle~

@ rect~
syntax: rect~ [freq] [duty]
Band-limited pulse (PolyBLEP), from -1 to 1. The right inlet sets the duty
cycle, the fraction of the period spent at 1 (clamped to 0.01-0.99); both
inlets take signals or floats.
arg freq: the frequency in Hz (0)
arg duty: the duty cycle, 0-1 (0.5)
see: saw~ tri~ cycle~

@ tri~
syntax: tri~ [freq] [slope]
Triangle wave from -1 to 1. The slope is where in the period the wave turns
from rising to falling (clamped to 0.01-0.99): 0.5 is symmetric, values near
0 or 1 make a sawtooth. Both inlets take signals or floats.
arg freq: the frequency in Hz (0)
arg slope: the turning point, 0-1 (0.5)
see: saw~ rect~ cycle~

@ noise~
syntax: noise~
White noise between -1 and 1. Each noise~ has its own seed, so two of them
are uncorrelated.
see: cycle~ reson~ svf~ nebula~

@ click~
syntax: click~ [shape...]
Plays an impulse each time it gets a bang: by default a single sample of 1,
or the shape given as arguments or as a list of numbers (optionally after
`set`). An empty list returns to the single sample.
arg shape: the samples of the impulse (1)
msg bang: plays the impulse
msg set values...: sets the impulse shape (a list of numbers alone does the same)
see: line~ comb~ reson~ sig~

@ sig~
syntax: sig~ [value]
Turns a number into a constant signal. A signal in its inlet passes through
unchanged.
arg value: the starting value (0)
see: line~ snapshot~ +~

@ line~
syntax: line~ [value]
Signal ramps. A list of target-time pairs makes segments: `1 10 0 300` goes
to 1 in 10 ms, then to 0 in 300 ms (up to 64 segments). A float alone jumps
to that value, or ramps to it over the time last sent to the right inlet,
which is used once and then reset to 0. A new message drops the segments
still pending and starts from the current value.
arg value: the starting value (0)
msg value [ms]: jumps or ramps to the value
msg target ms target ms ...: a sequence of ramps
msg stop: freezes the ramp where it is
see: line function sig~ gain~

@ nebula~
syntax: nebula~ [proto1] [proto2]
Granular synthesis (sound_math's nebula): clouds of windowed grains read from
two prototypes, each a waveform (`sine`, `triangle`, `square`, `noise`) or the
name of a buffer~, mixed by `morph`. A grain starts every 1/density seconds,
lasts `length` ms and is placed at random in the stereo field; the outlets
are left and right.

For a waveform the frequency is in Hz; for a buffer~ it is a speed ratio
(1 = as recorded), unless `pitch1` or `pitch2` gives the sound's pitch, so
that a waveform and a sound can be morphed at the same pitch. The second
number after density, length, freq and jitter is randomness, as a ratio of
the value: `density 40 .5` gives 20 to 60 grains a second. The inlets after
the left one take density, length, frequency and morph as floats.
arg proto1: the first prototype: sine, triangle, square, noise or a buffer~ name (sine)
arg proto2: the second prototype (as proto1)
msg 1/0: starts or stops the cloud (also start, bang, stop)
msg density d [r]: grains per second (10), and its randomness 0-0.99 (0)
msg length ms [r]: grain duration in ms (100), and its randomness 0-1 (0)
msg freq f [r]: grain frequency, Hz or ratio (220), and its randomness 0-1 (0)
msg morph m: the mix of the prototypes, 0 = proto1, 1 = proto2 (0)
msg amp a: the amplitude of each grain (0.25)
msg gliss g: each grain glides to its frequency times 1 +- g, at random, 0-1 (0)
msg jitter j [r]: stereo position, 0 = right, 1 = left (0.5), and its randomness 0-1 (1)
msg speed s: how fast the read position moves through a buffer~ (1)
msg grainlets n: each grain lasts n periods of its frequency, replacing length; 0 = off (0)
msg window type: the grain window: hann, hamming or blackman (hann)
msg poly n: the most grains at once, 1-1024 (256)
msg proto1 name: changes the first prototype (proto2 the second)
msg pitch1 hz: the pitch of a buffer~ prototype, so its frequency is in Hz; 0 returns to ratios (pitch2 for the second)
msg seed n: restarts the random generator with this seed
see: buffer~ groove~ sparkle~ noise~ cycle~

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Filters

@ reson~
syntax: reson~ [gain] [cf] [Q]
Two-pole resonant bandpass with the structure of Max's reson~, scaled so that
its gain at the centre frequency is exactly `gain`. The parameter inlets take
signals or floats but are read once per 64-sample block; a list `gain cf Q`
in the left inlet sets all three.
arg gain: the peak gain (1)
arg cf: the centre frequency in Hz (1000)
arg Q: the centre frequency over the bandwidth: higher is narrower (10)
msg gain cf Q: sets the three parameters
msg clear: empties the filter's memory
see: lores~ svf~ biquad~ filterdesign noise~

@ lores~
syntax: lores~ [cf] [res]
Resonant low-pass (an RBJ cookbook biquad whose Q rises from 0.7 to about 25
with the resonance). The cutoff and resonance inlets take signals or floats,
read once per block.
arg cf: the cutoff frequency in Hz (1000)
arg res: the resonance, 0-1 (0)
msg clear: empties the filter's memory
see: svf~ reson~ biquad~ filterdesign

@ svf~
syntax: svf~ [freq] [q]
State-variable filter (topology-preserving) with four outlets: low-pass,
high-pass, band-pass and notch. The frequency and Q inlets take signals or
floats, read once per block.
arg freq: the cutoff or centre frequency in Hz (1000)
arg q: the resonance, 0-1 (0.5)
see: lores~ reson~ biquad~ filterdesign

@ biquad~
syntax: biquad~ [a0] [a1] [a2] [b1] [b2]
Two-pole two-zero filter in Max's convention:
`y = a0 x + a1 x[n-1] + a2 x[n-2] - b1 y[n-1] - b2 y[n-2]`. The coefficient
inlets take floats, not signals; a list of five in the left inlet sets them
all, as filterdesign outputs it. The defaults pass the signal unchanged.
arg a0: the gain of the input (1)
arg a1: the gain of the input one sample before (0)
arg a2: the gain of the input two samples before (0)
arg b1: the gain of the output one sample before, subtracted (0)
arg b2: the gain of the output two samples before, subtracted (0)
msg a0 a1 a2 b1 b2: sets the five coefficients
see: filterdesign filterplot lores~ svf~

@ filterdesign
syntax: filterdesign [type] [freq] [q] [gain]
Computes the coefficients of a biquad~ for a filter type, from Robert
Bristow-Johnson's Audio EQ Cookbook (as Musil's biquad): lowpass, highpass,
bandpass, notch (or bandstop), peak, lowshelf, highshelf, allpass. A type, a
frequency (left inlet), a Q (middle) or a gain in dB (right; used by peak and
the shelves) outputs the list `a0 a1 a2 b1 b2`, for biquad~ and filterplot.
The coefficients follow the current sample rate (44100 while audio is off).
arg type: the filter type (lowpass)
arg freq: the frequency in Hz (1000)
arg q: the Q (0.707)
arg gain: the gain in dB, for peak and the shelves (0)
msg type: sets the type and outputs
msg freq [q] [gain]: sets the frequency (and Q and gain) and outputs
msg bang: outputs the coefficients again
see: biquad~ filterplot lores~ svf~

@ filterplot
syntax: filterplot [width] [height]
Draws the frequency response, in dB from +24 to -48 on a logarithmic
frequency axis, of the biquad~ coefficients it receives. A list of 10, 15 ...
numbers is a cascade of biquads in series. Dragging in it (locked) outputs
`frequency gain` (Hz, dB) at the mouse, to drive a filterdesign.
arg width: the width in pixels, 120-2000 (300)
arg height: the height in pixels, 60-1000 (140)
msg a0 a1 a2 b1 b2 ...: the coefficients to draw
see: filterdesign biquad~ svf~

@ slide~
syntax: slide~ [up] [down]
Smooths a signal: `y += (x - y) / slide`, where the slide is `up` samples
while the input is above the output and `down` while it is below; 1 passes
the signal through. Useful for envelope followers and portamento. The slide
inlets take signals or floats.
arg up: the slide while rising, in samples (1)
arg down: the slide while falling, in samples (1)
msg reset: jumps to the input
see: line~ lores~ abs~ sah~

@ conv~
syntax: conv~ buffer [channels]
Convolves each inlet with the impulse response in a buffer~, with no
latency: the first 256 taps directly, the rest by partitioned FFT. Channel c
uses the response's channel c, or its last one; an inlet with nothing
connected takes the channel before, so one source into `conv~ hall 2` gives a
stereo reverb. The response is normalised to unit energy unless `normalize
0`. It is reloaded when the buffer~ changes.
arg buffer: the buffer~ holding the impulse response (required)
arg channels: the number of inlets and outlets, 1-8 (1)
msg set name: uses another buffer~
msg gain x: scales the response (1)
msg normalize 0/1: normalises the response to unit energy, or not (1)
see: buffer~ condec reverb~

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Signal Math

@ +~ -~ *~ /~
syntax: +~ [value]
Add, subtract, multiply or divide two signals, sample by sample. A float in
either inlet is used as a constant while no signal is connected there; the
argument sets the right one. Division by 0 gives 0.
arg value: the right operand (0)
see: expr~ gain~ sig~ clip~

@ gain~
syntax: gain~ [ramp] [amplitude]
A fader that scales the signal in its left inlet. It runs in dB from silence
to +6 dB (amplitude 0 to 2) and shows its level in dB below; every change
ramps linearly over `ramp` ms, so moving it never clicks. A float in the left
inlet sets the amplitude (1 = unity), the right inlet the ramp time. The
right outlet reports the amplitude when the fader is moved. The ramp and the
amplitude are saved with the patch.
arg ramp: the ramp time in ms (20)
arg amplitude: the starting amplitude, 0-2 (1)
msg amplitude: sets the amplitude, without output
msg bang: outputs the amplitude
see: *~ line~ meter~ dac~

@ clip~
syntax: clip~ [min] [max]
Limits a signal to the range min..max. The limits are signals or floats.
arg min: the lower limit (-1)
arg max: the upper limit (1)
see: tanh~ abs~ expr~

@ abs~
syntax: abs~
The absolute value of a signal (full-wave rectification).
see: clip~ slide~ tanh~

@ tanh~
syntax: tanh~
Soft saturation: the hyperbolic tangent of a signal, which stays within -1
and 1. Scale the input up to drive it harder.
see: clip~ abs~ expr~

@ expr~
syntax: expr~ expression
An expr evaluated for every sample. `$v1` to `$v9` read the inlets, which
take signals or floats (a float is held while nothing is connected); the
highest one used sets the number of inlets. `$f` and `$i` read them too,
`$i` truncated. Operators: `+ - * / %`, comparisons, `&& || !`, `pi`;
functions: sin cos tan asin acos atan sinh cosh tanh exp log log10 sqrt abs
floor ceil int round, pow atan2 min max fmod random(lo, hi). A result that
is not a finite number gives 0; errors are printed in the console.
arg expression: the expression, e.g. `$v1 * (1 - $v3) + $v2 * $v3`
see: expr +~ *~ clip~

@ sah~
syntax: sah~ [threshold]
Sample and hold: each time the right signal rises above the threshold, the
current value of the left signal is held at the outlet until the next rise.
A float in the right inlet sets the threshold; in the left inlet, a constant
input.
arg threshold: the level the trigger must cross upwards (0)
see: phasor~ noise~ snapshot~ slide~

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Signal Routing

@ selector~
syntax: selector~ n [initial]
Passes one of `n` signal inlets to the outlet; a number in the left inlet
picks it, 1 to `n`, or 0 for none. Unlike Max's, it crossfades over 5 ms,
so switching never clicks.
arg n: the number of signal inlets, 1-64 (1)
arg initial: the inlet passed at first, 0 = none (0)
msg n: picks the inlet (left inlet)
see: gate~ gate *~ send~

@ gate~
syntax: gate~ n [initial]
Sends the signal in its right inlet to one of `n` outlets, picked by a number
in the left inlet (1 to `n`, 0 = closed); the others output silence. It
crossfades over 5 ms, so switching never clicks.
arg n: the number of outlets, 1-64 (1)
arg initial: the outlet open at first, 0 = closed (0)
msg n: picks the outlet (left inlet)
see: selector~ gate send~

@ send~
syntax: send~ name
Makes its signal available to every receive~ of the same name, in any
subpatcher of the patch. The chain runs send~ before its receive~s, so there
is no delay, except around a feedback loop through them (one 64-sample
block).
arg name: the name (required)
msg set name: changes the name
see: receive~ send tapin~ dac~

@ receive~
syntax: receive~ name
Outputs the sum of the signals of every send~ with its name.
arg name: the name (required)
msg set name: listens to another name
see: send~ receive adc~

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Delay

@ tapin~
syntax: tapin~ [max]
Writes its input into a delay line, read by the tapout~ connected to its
outlet. The memory is sized for 96 kHz, so at lower rates delays a little
beyond `max` still work. Since the link does not order the two,
tapout~ -> ... -> tapin~ feedback is allowed.
arg max: the longest delay in ms, 1-60000 (1000)
msg clear: empties the delay line
see: tapout~ comb~ allpass~ send~

@ tapout~
syntax: tapout~ [ms...]
Reads the delay line of the tapin~ connected to its left inlet, one tap per
argument, each with its own inlet (signal or float) and outlet. Delays are
in ms, interpolated, and limited by the tapin~'s length; inside a feedback
loop the shortest delay is one block (64 samples).
arg ms: the delay of each tap in ms (one tap, 0)
see: tapin~ comb~ allpass~

@ comb~
syntax: comb~ [max] [delay] [a] [b] [c]
Comb filter: `y = a x + b x[n-D] + c y[n-D]`, where D is the delay. The
delay and the three gains are signals or floats; the memory is sized for
96 kHz, as with tapin~.
arg max: the longest delay in ms, 1-10000 (50)
arg delay: the delay in ms (half of max)
arg a: the gain of the input (1)
arg b: the gain of the delayed input (0)
arg c: the feedback gain (0.5)
msg clear: empties the delay lines
see: allpass~ tapin~ reverb~ reson~

@ allpass~
syntax: allpass~ [max] [delay] [gain]
Allpass filter: `y = -g x + x[n-D] + g y[n-D]`; it changes the phase but not
the spectrum of a steady sound, and is a building block of reverbs. The
delay and gain are signals or floats.
arg max: the longest delay in ms, 1-10000 (50)
arg delay: the delay in ms (half of max)
arg gain: the gain g (0.7)
msg clear: empties the delay lines
see: comb~ tapin~ reverb~ fvcomb~

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- I/O

@ adc~
syntax: adc~ [ch...]
Audio input: one outlet per channel given, numbered from 1. The input device
is opened with all its channels (Patch > Audio Setup can limit them), so
adc~ 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 reads an eight-channel interface; a channel the device
does not have outputs silence. The input opens only when a patch contains
adc~.
arg ch: the input channels (1 2)
msg start: turns audio on (also startwindow)
msg stop: turns audio off
see: dac~ dspstate~ meter~ record~

@ dac~
syntax: dac~ [ch...]
Audio output: one inlet per channel given, numbered from 1. Several dac~s
add up on the same channel. The output device is opened with all its
channels (Patch > Audio Setup can limit them), so dac~ 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 plays
on eight speakers. Channels beyond the device's are not heard, but the
toolbar's meter shows them and the quick recorder writes them, and --render
and moritz render them: an eight-channel piece can be made on a stereo
card. A console note says when this happens.
arg ch: the output channels (1 2)
msg start: turns audio on (also startwindow)
msg stop: turns audio off
see: adc~ gain~ dspstate~ meter~

@ dspstate~
syntax: dspstate~ [ms]
Reports the audio state on bang, whenever audio starts, stops or is
reopened, and every `ms` while audio runs. Its outlets, left to right, are as
in Max: 1/0 for audio on or off, the sample rate, the signal vector size (64,
or the frame's bin count inside pfft~) and the I/O buffer size; a fifth gives
the CPU load in %. While audio is off it reports the last sample rate and
buffer size.
arg ms: the reporting interval in ms (at least 20), 0 = only on bang and on changes (0)
msg bang: reports now
msg ms: sets the interval (also interval ms)
see: adc~ dac~ loadbang
