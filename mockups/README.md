# Mockups: patches, sounds, scores and timelines

These are design sketches, not screenshots. They show how a sound editor, a score
(with its roll and timeline) and lists of data could live next to the patch in Moritz
without one replacing the others. Nothing here is implemented yet.

## The idea in one paragraph

Moritz has four kinds of things:

- **Patches** are processes.
- **Scores** are things placed in time.
- **Sounds** are samples.
- **Data** is lists of lists, held in a `coll`.

Patches and scores contain each other at any depth:

- A patch can hold a `score` object.
- A score can hold patches as events, instruments or effects.
- A score can hold other scores; this is the recursion.

Sounds are leaves. Data is the material passed between the others.

There is no fixed top level: the file you open is the top. Open a patch and you work as in
Max (mockup 2). Open a score and you work as in a DAW (mockup 4). Every editor is a view of
one of these four things, so closing a view never loses anything.

## The lifecycle (mockup 7)

1. **Collect.** Record with the Quick Recorder or `sfrecord~`, load files, import MIDI.
2. **Clean and mark** in the sound editor: trim, fade, denoise. Add markers from onsets,
   beats or novelty, or regions by hand.
3. **Analyse** in a patch: soundtypes units, pursuit books, MIR features, later Orchidea
   targets. The results go into a coll or a buffer~.
4. **Generate** a score from a patch, or with the roll's Generate panel: typegen, Markov
   chains, Musil's textures, pivots, walks and row forms, Orchidea solutions.
5. **Edit the score** by hand in the roll, the piano roll or the text view.
6. **Arrange** in the timeline: place scores, sounds and patches, make instances, draw
   automation.
7. **Sound design.** Track instruments and effects are patches, edited live while the
   piece plays.
8. **Perform or render.** Play live on the transport, or render offline in stereo,
   N channels, binaural or stems. MusicXML and MIDI can be exported.
9. **Back to material.** A render is a new sound: it goes back into a buffer~, a dictionary
   or a new score.
