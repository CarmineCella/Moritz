# Mockups: patches, sounds, scores and timelines

These are design sketches, not screenshots. They show how a sound editor, a score
(with its roll and timeline) and lists of data could live next to the patch in Moritz
without one replacing the others. The sound editor is being built for 0.12; the rest is
not implemented yet. The decisions and the plan are at the end.

## The idea in one paragraph

Moritz has four kinds of things:

- **Patches** are processes.
- **Scores** are things placed in time.
- **Sounds** are samples.
- **Data** is lists of lists, held in a `coll`.

Patches and scores contain each other at any depth:

- A patch can hold a `score` object.
- A score can hold patches as events, instruments or effects.
- A score can hold other scores; this is the recursion.

Sounds are leaves. Data is the material passed between the others.

There is no fixed top level: the file you open is the top. Open a patch and you work as in
Max (mockup 2). Open a score and you work as in a DAW (mockup 4). Every editor is a view of
one of these four things, so closing a view never loses anything.

## The lifecycle (mockup 7)

1. **Collect.** Record with the Quick Recorder or `sfrecord~`, load files, import MIDI.
2. **Clean and mark** in the sound editor: trim, fade, denoise. Add markers from onsets,
   beats or novelty, or regions by hand.
3. **Analyse** in a patch: soundtypes units, pursuit books, MIR features, later Orchidea
   targets. The results go into a coll or a buffer~.
4. **Generate** a score from a patch, or with the roll's Generate panel: typegen, Markov
   chains, Musil's textures, pivots, walks and row forms, Orchidea solutions.
5. **Edit the score** by hand in the roll, the piano roll or the text view.
6. **Arrange** in the timeline: place scores, sounds and patches, make instances, draw
   automation.
7. **Sound design.** Track instruments and effects are patches, edited live while the
   piece plays.
8. **Perform or render.** Play live on the transport, or render offline in stereo,
   N channels, binaural or stems. MusicXML and MIDI can be exported.
9. **Back to material.** A render is a new sound: it goes back into a buffer~, a dictionary
   or a new score.

The images are generated by a script (kept outside the repository) from Moritz's colours
and real sounds from `examples/sounds`.

## Decisions (November 2026)

- **Timeline and roll are views of one thing, the score.** A score holds notes (played by an
  instrument: SOL samples or a patch), sounds (regions of a file or a buffer~), patches (that run
  while their clip plays, with automated parameters) and other scores, at any depth. A score cannot
  contain itself. The sound editor is the view of sounds, the leaves.
- **The score is one C++ structure, defined in Musil's C++** (a header next to Musil's other
  engines, depending neither on the interpreter nor on Moritz). Musil uses it as a value: the
  functions of `music.mu` work on it, and Musil alone still plays scores live. Moritz includes the
  same header and adds editing with undo, sample-accurate playback (a time-ordered plan handed to the
  audio thread, as for the signal graph) and the views. Orchidea, typegen and the score generators
  produce it directly.
- **A .score file is Musil data**: the score written as a list of lists, as in mockup 5. Musil reads
  it as a value; Moritz opens it without running code (safe for files from elsewhere) and writes it
  back after every edit. A script that generates a score is ordinary Musil code; what it produces is
  saved as data. A score converts to and from a coll (one event per row) for its flat parts.
- **Musil is the offline language in Moritz.** Moritz's audio thread runs only Moritz's C++. Musil
  generates, transforms and renders; mockups 10 to 14 (the language, the code editor, the REPL, code
  in patches) stay valid in this sense: patches and scores are C++ structures of Moritz shown to the
  language as values.
- **`score` is an active object** (mockup 2): edited inside its box, driven by messages, played on
  the transport, its events sent from its outlets. Double-click opens it large, as timeline or roll
  (the same panel).
- **Each editor is written once, as a panel** (mockup 8), first in its own window.

## Plan

- **0.12: the sound editor** (mockup 3, without spectral painting): overview, waveform and
  spectrogram, zoom, selection and preview playback, edits with undo, save; then the Process menu
  (offline objects and patches applied to the selection), markers and regions.
- **0.13: the score and the timeline** (mockup 4): the score in Musil's C++ and `music.mu` moved
  onto it (today's scripts giving the same results), the .score file, `transport` and the clock,
  playback in Moritz, then the timeline: sound clips (from the editor's regions), patch clips,
  automation lanes, tracks with instruments and effects, offline render. Note clips are shown and
  moved as blocks; their notes are edited with the roll.
- **0.14: the roll and the active `score` object** (mockups 5 and 2): editing notes, the inline
  score box, the Generate and Transform panel (Musil offline), coll (mockup 9).
- **Later:** notation, MusicXML import, spectral painting, the patch dock (layout C), and the parts
  of mockups 10 to 14 not done by then.

Each version is worked on in its own branch, on macOS, validated by hand; CI runs once, when the
version is ready, before it is merged into main and tagged.
